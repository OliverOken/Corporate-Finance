Company Overview
Lunit Inc. (328130.KS, KOSDAQ) is a South Korean medical AI company specializing in diagnostic imaging solutions. The company develops AI-powered software for chest X-ray analysis, mammography screening, and digital pathology, with FDA-cleared products deployed across healthcare systems globally. Trading at approximately KRW 1.2 trillion market capitalization, Lunit reports in Korean Won under K-IFRS standards with a December 31 fiscal year end.

Selection Rationale
Lunit represents a compelling intersection of healthcare innovation, international expansion, and emerging market technology leadership. As AI transforms medical diagnostics, Lunit's regulatory approvals (FDA, CE mark) and commercial partnerships with GE Healthcare and Fujifilm position it uniquely among Asian healthcare technology companies. The company's transition from R&D-focused startup to revenue-generating enterprise with global operations creates rich analytical opportunities around profitability inflection, international cash flow management, and technology commercialization efficiency.

For ratio analysis, Lunit offers several compelling dynamics: rapid international expansion requiring significant working capital, R&D-intensive operations typical of AI companies, and a licensing/SaaS revenue model that should demonstrate improving unit economics as scale increases.

Data Availability & Sources
Financial data will be sourced from Lunit's English-language annual reports (available via company IR page at lunit.io), supplemented by KOSDAQ regulatory filings. The company reports under K-IFRS standards, facilitating comparison with international healthcare technology peers. Yahoo Finance and Bloomberg will provide market data for ratio calculations. Lunit's transparent disclosure practices and English reporting accommodate comprehensive financial statement analysis.

Preliminary Observations
I expect declining R&D intensity ratios from FY2023 → FY2024 because Lunit's core AI algorithms have achieved regulatory approval, shifting focus from pure research to commercial deployment and incremental product enhancement.

I expect improving working capital efficiency because the company's SaaS licensing model generates upfront payments while reducing inventory and manufacturing capital requirements compared to traditional medical device companies.

I expect increasing international revenue concentration to strain short-term liquidity ratios because global expansion requires significant upfront sales and marketing investment before recurring revenue materializes, particularly in regulated healthcare markets.

Ratio Categories Preview
Profitability ratios are paramount given Lunit's transition from R&D to commercialization. Efficiency ratios will reveal technology monetization effectiveness, while liquidity ratios matter critically for funding international expansion without equity dilution. Leverage ratios remain relevant as the company balances growth investment with financial stability.

Data Collection Plan
Analysis requires three years of income statements, balance sheets, and cash flow statements from annual reports. Market assumptions (risk-free rates, sector multiples) will be sourced from Korean and US financial markets. Currency considerations include KRW reporting with USD revenue exposure requiring foreign exchange impact analysis on margin calculations.

Notable Financial Observations:

Lunit operates at a significant net operating loss, with negative NOPAT, despite generating revenues of ₩83.1 billion. This is due to the company's heavy investments in R&D and international expansion.
Lunit's WACC is estimated at 6.4%, with a Cost of Equity range of 5.2% to 7.9% and an assumed Cost of Debt near 5.0%.
Given the negative NOPAT, Lunit's Economic Value Added (EVA) is also negative, indicating the company is not generating sufficient returns to cover its cost of capital.
However, Lunit has formally pivoted toward aggressive cost efficiency, with a stated target of reaching EBITDA break-even and fundamental profitability.
