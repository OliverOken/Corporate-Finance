## 1. Executive Summary

Lunit Inc. (KOSDAQ: 328130) is a South Korean medical artificial intelligence company that develops AI-powered cancer imaging diagnostics and biomarker analytics software. Its products are deployed across more than 10,000 hospitals in over 50 countries, supported by regulatory clearances from the U.S. FDA and CE marks under EU MDR.

**Key finding:** Lunit's operating economics are improving rapidly — revenue grew 53% to ₩83,100M in FY2025 — but the company's financing structure, inherited from its 2024 acquisition of New Zealand-based Volpara Health Technologies, is the single largest driver of its financial distress. The most important ratio to understand Lunit's current position is the **debt burden ratio of 8.70x**: it reveals that for every ₩1 of operating loss, financing costs amplify the net loss nearly nine-fold. Market investors, however, are pricing in the growth story — the market-to-book ratio of 5.20x signals that the option value of Lunit's global AI platform far exceeds its current book equity.

## 4. Du Pont Decomposition

The **Du Pont decomposition** is an analytical framework that breaks return on equity into component drivers, allowing analysts to identify which aspect of a business — profitability, efficiency, leverage, or financing — is responsible for the overall return. The four-factor version used here is:

`ROE = Leverage × Asset Turnover × Operating Profit Margin × Debt Burden`

| Factor | Named Range | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Leverage (equity multiplier) | `RATIO_leverage` | 2.69x |
| Asset turnover | `RATIO_asset_turnover` | 0.191x |
| Operating profit margin | `RATIO_operating_profit_margin` | −6.6% |
| Debt burden | `RATIO_debt_burden` | 8.70x |
| **ROE (Du Pont)** | | **−29.4%** |

Computed: `2.69 × 0.191 × (−0.066) × 8.70 = −29.4%`

**Primary driver:** The operating profit margin (−6.6%) is the sign-driver — it is the factor that makes ROE negative. Without a negative margin, leverage and debt burden would amplify a positive return. However, the **debt burden (8.70x)** is the magnitude amplifier: it scales the modest operating loss into the severe net loss that produces a −29.4% ROE. The strategic implication is that fixing the debt burden (through refinancing or debt reduction) would have a larger near-term impact on ROE than further improving the operating margin — though both are necessary for long-run value creation.

**Asset turnover (0.19x)** is a drag but structurally bounded by the goodwill-heavy asset base. **Leverage (2.69x)** is a moderate equity multiplier that amplifies in the wrong direction given negative operating margins.

**Time-mismatch disclosure:** The Du Pont ROE (−29.4%) differs from the directly computed ROE (−28.7%) by 0.7 percentage points. This discrepancy is structural, not an error. `RATIO_leverage` uses current-year (end-FY2025) balance sheet figures, while `RATIO_asset_turnover` uses prior-year (end-FY2024) assets. This mixing of time periods is inherent to the model design specified in the technical specification, and the resulting discrepancy has been verified and documented.

## 5. Strategic Recommendations

---

> **Recommendation 1: Refinance Short-Term Debt into Long-Term Obligations**
>
> *Evidence:* Current ratio (0.26x); short-term debt of ₩93,369M representing 44% of total current liabilities; cash balance of ₩23,668.4M covering only 11% of current liabilities (cash ratio: 0.11x).
>
> *Action:* Engage creditors and capital markets to convert the ₩93,369M of current debt into long-term obligations with maturities of three to five years. This could be structured as a refinancing of the Volpara acquisition-related convertible bonds that have or are approaching maturity. A successful refinancing would move short-term debt off the current liabilities balance, dramatically improving all liquidity ratios.
>
> *Expected ratio impact:* Current ratio would improve from 0.26x toward or above 1.0x. Net working capital to assets would move from −42.3% toward positive territory. Immediate solvency risk would be substantially reduced.

---

> **Recommendation 2: Prioritize Interest Expense Reduction**
>
> *Evidence:* Debt burden (8.70x); times interest earned (−1.57x); interest expense of ₩53,100M vs. after-tax operating income of −₩5,451M; net margin of −57.0% vs. operating margin of −6.6%.
>
> *Action:* Allocate a portion of any future equity proceeds (including from the ₩250B rights offering completed in FY2025) to paying down the highest-coupon acquisition-related debt. Even a ₩20,000–30,000M reduction in outstanding debt principal would reduce annual interest by an estimated ₩1,500–2,500M at prevailing Korean credit rates, materially narrowing the gap between operating income and net income.
>
> *Expected ratio impact:* Debt burden would decline from 8.70x toward 3–5x. Net margin would improve from −57.0% toward −30% to −40% range. ROE (direct) would improve from −28.7% toward −15% to −20%.

---

> **Recommendation 3: Accelerate Revenue to Cross Operating Breakeven**
>
> *Evidence:* Operating profit margin of −6.6% on ₩83,100M revenue; 53% revenue growth in FY2025; COGS + SGA + D&A of approximately ₩166,200M implying fixed-cost exposure that can be leveraged.
>
> *Action:* Maintain current revenue growth trajectory (53% YoY in FY2025). At this rate, revenue could reach ₩127,000M+ in FY2026. If COGS and SGA contain a meaningful fixed component (reasonable for a software platform), the incremental revenue margin should exceed average margin — pushing the operating profit margin toward zero. The Volpara Health breast imaging product in the U.S. market (200+ hospitals already deployed) represents the most scalable near-term revenue lever.
>
> *Expected ratio impact:* Operating profit margin would improve from −6.6% toward 0% to +5%. After-tax operating income would turn positive, EVA would improve, and ROA and ROC would move toward zero.

---

> **Recommendation 4: Improve Receivables Collection to Reduce Short-Term Borrowing Dependency**
>
> *Evidence:* Average collection period of 95.5 days; operating cash flow of −₩124,616M; cash balance declining ₩60,450.5M year-over-year.
>
> *Action:* Negotiate payment terms with hospital system customers to target a 70–75 day average collection period. Introduce early-payment incentives for large contract customers. Implement automated billing and AR follow-up processes. A 20-day reduction in DSO (days sales outstanding) on ₩83,100M revenue would free approximately ₩4,552M of cash (20 × ₩227.7M daily sales).
>
> *Expected ratio impact:* Receivables turnover would improve from 3.82x toward 5.0x. Cash ratio would improve modestly. Reduced cash consumption would decrease reliance on short-term borrowing and support the Recommendation 1 refinancing strategy.

---

> **Recommendation 5: Monitor Goodwill for Impairment Risk**
>
> *Evidence:* Intangible assets (goodwill) of ₩278,704.5M = 75.3% of total assets; market-to-book of 5.20x; equity of ₩137,432.6M.
>
> *Action:* Conduct a rigorous annual impairment test of the Volpara Health goodwill under K-IFRS IAS 36 criteria. The test should stress-test integration milestones, U.S. hospital deployment targets, and revenue contribution from the acquired business. If Volpara revenue growth falls materially short of acquisition-case projections, a partial write-down should be disclosed proactively rather than delayed.
>
> *Expected ratio impact:* A goodwill write-down of ₩50,000–100,000M would reduce total assets and equity by equal amounts. Total debt ratio would rise (toward 70%+). Leverage ratio would increase. Market-to-book could paradoxically improve (lower book). However, the operating income statement impact would be a one-time non-cash charge — TIE and cash coverage would be unaffected.

## 6. Conclusion

Lunit's FY2025 financial profile presents a company with a strong commercial narrative and a challenged financial structure. Revenue grew 53% to ₩83,100M, and market investors value the company at 5.20x book equity — confident that the global medical AI platform will generate returns commensurate with its ₩278,704.5M goodwill asset. At the operating level, the evidence supports cautious optimism: the after-tax operating margin of −6.6% is narrowing, and the path to operating breakeven is visible if current growth rates hold.

The most critical near-term risk is liquidity and short-term debt. With a current ratio of 0.26x, ₩93,369M of current-portion debt, and operating cash flow of −₩124,616M, Lunit is dependent on continued access to capital markets. A tightening of credit conditions or a failed refinancing of current debt obligations would materially threaten the company's ability to execute on its growth strategy. Debt restructuring — specifically converting short-term obligations into long-term instruments — is the single highest-priority financial action available to management.
